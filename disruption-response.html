Emergency activation

Get on the critical path.

When an event hits, restoring the site is only part of the problem. Roads may remain closed, utilities unavailable, suppliers unable to deliver, or public infrastructure may take weeks to recover. Terranova assesses how these constraints affects the connection to the wider territory, helping the team determine what to address and how.

01

Establish what is affected

Assessment of the current status of the site and the territorial connections it depends on: access routes, utilities, suppliers, etc. Each is recorded as Operational,Partially Operational, Inoperative, Not Verified, based on the available information.

02

Identify what is blocking recovery

A damaged road does not necessarly stop operation. An unavailable acces route with no alternatives might. We identify which dependencies are critical to restarting and which constraints need to be solved first.

03

Asses viable alternatives

We examine possible alternatives where a critical connection is unavailable. Different routes, logistics facilities, temporary operating arrengements or relocations are part of the scope. The aim is to find ways to restart or mantain the activity running.

04

Account for public recovery

Some constraints can be addressed by the company. Others depend on infrastructure operators, public authorities or wider regional recovery. We distinguish between the two, assessing what is known and identifying the decisions that your team can take.

What you get

Know when operations can restart — and where to deploy capital first.

You get the status of what is affected, still functioning or still unverified; the constraints, owners and timing on the critical path; and a sequenced set of actions for restoration, temporary continuity or relocation.

  • Disruption and dependency status — a structured view of affected, functioning and unverified dependencies, including the evidence behind each status.
  • Critical-path and recovery timeline — the constraints blocking recovery, the responsible parties, and the expected timing or conditions for resolution.
  • Prioritised recovery actions — a sequenced set of actions, with options and decision points for restoration, temporary continuity or relocation.

Every material delay sits on one of two tracks.

Government and public organisations

What cannot be fixed directly. Roads, utilities and public works follow the region's recovery plan.

  • Public infrastructure restoration
  • Authority and operator timetables
  • Constraints that have to be waited on

Companies

What can be acted on now. This is where capital compresses downtime.

  • Alternative routes and suppliers

What the timelines may recommend.

Stabilise, temporary continuity or relocate — after the timeframes, not before.

Stabilise & restore

Recover current operations when the critical constraints can be removed in time.

Temporary continuity

Alternative routes, warehouses or facilities so the operation does not wait on the site.

Relocate

Temporary or permanent, when the site cannot carry continuity in time — same triggers and constraints, as a module.

On-site option

When remote intake is not enough, we can place a team on the ground to validate damage, access, utilities and the dependencies that still hold the operation.

  • Confirm what is hit — and what still runs — where the maps and calls leave gaps.
  • Read access, power, logistics and public works priorities in the field.
  • Feed the reactivation timelines with observed status, and local coordination.
