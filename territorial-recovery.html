The territory after disruption is not the one that existed before.

When shaping the recovery, the question is not simply what to restore, but what to restore first, what to redesign, and where investment will unlock the most. We turn those choices into a sequenced recovery strategy based on cost, speed, and long-term value — reconstruction as territorial strategy, with a path toward territorial and regional development rather than a return to the previous map.

  1. Preventive Territorial Exposure Assessment — preparing against disruption and increasing territorial resilience.
  2. Reactive Operational Disruption Response — emergency time-to-reactivation and capital deployment plan.
  3. Proactive Strategic Territorial Recovery — planning of permanent territorial reset while improving the affected area.

A sequence of decisions, not a list of projects.

Not everything can recover at once, and not everything should return as it was. We connect what still works, temporary solutions and permanent reconstruction into one sequence — so time and capital go to the decisions that unlock the next.

Picture

Read the disrupted territory: what is damaged, what still works, and how the area can function next. Sector impact across mobility, services, energy, finance, and social and cultural recovery.

Triggers

Identify the interventions that unlock the fastest recovery. Assess each affected element against its role in the recovery chain — relocate, expand, rebuild, or abandon — and determine which interventions unlock the greatest number of subsequent actions.

Prioritise & sequence

Build the recovery domino. Sequence interventions against expected timing and cost, defining what needs to happen first, what follows, and what each stage requires. Turn the recovery into a clear path from first intervention to full territorial recovery.

What you get

Know what goes first — and what each element becomes.

A single decision framework for the territory’s recovery. Every intervention is connected to its wider consequences, making priorities traceable, decisions easier to coordinate, and investment easier to sequence — from the first intervention through to the territory’s long-term reset.

Intervention prioritisation

Score each recovery intervention according to its territorial recovery impact and unlock potential. The resulting score establishes what goes first, what follows, and what can wait.

  • Intervention map — affected and unaffected elements across sectors, brought into one recovery picture.
  • Priority score — a ranking based on territorial impact and recovery potential.
  • Recovery sequence — the order of intervention, showing what each priority unlocks for the next.

Every intervention is scored on a priority scale.

1 — Critical catalyst

Highest recovery and unlock potential.

2 — High priority

Significant territorial consequences.

3 — Strategic priority

Important to the wider recovery, but not an initial catalyst.

4 — Secondary

Useful for recovery, with limited cascading effect.

5 — Low consequence

Limited impact on the wider territorial recovery.

From the same territorial picture, each element receives one reconstruction action.

Element action plan

Tag bridges, neighbourhoods, facilities and corridors by exposure, current condition and role in recovery. Not every element rebuilds — each gets one of five actions.

  • Element register — every asset in the recovery picture, by sector and current condition.
  • Action tag — one reconstruction action per element, based on exposure and recovery role.
  • Coordination view — every tag visible to authorities and stakeholders in one plan.

Every element is tagged with one of five actions.

Recover

Rebuild this link or neighbourhood now — it unlocks the rest of the area.

Temporary alternative

A military bridge or temporary facility can stay while permanent work waits.

Relocate

Move the function to unaffected capacity, and build the permanent asset once.

Expand

Do not restore the old footprint. Let the town or industrial area grow where it can work.

Do not rebuild

Some areas should not return. Capital is better spent unlocking what still functions.

When Territorial Strategy is crucial.

After a major disruption, every day counts. Our Strategic Territorial Recovery can considerably accelerate recovery and reactivation by focusing on what matters, when it matters.

Changing territorial conditions

Disruption changes how a territory functions. We help reassess its dependencies and needs to adapt recovery to the new reality.

Prioritising what matters

Setting priorities is difficult when everything feels urgent. We help establish where efforts can have the greatest impact for a more efficient recovery.

Sequencing interventions

The right intervention at the wrong time can delay recovery. We help structure intervention schedules to create the right sequence for faster and more coordinated development.

Aligning action

Recovery involves many actors, decisions and investments. We create a shared direction that aligns stakeholders around what needs to happen, when, and why.

Recovery as an opportunity.

Independent studies can help industrial groups, business holdings, and operators identify new territorial and industrial configurations and bring them to the table with authorities and public stakeholders. The result can go beyond recovery — turning reconstruction into a catalyst for regional development.
